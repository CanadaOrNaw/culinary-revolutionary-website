Confirming Availability: Service Area, Date and Guest Count

By Culinary Revolutionary · October 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Availability comes down to three facts: where, when, and how many. Here is how to confirm each before you plan further.

Before comparing menus or formats, confirm the basics: can the chef cook where you are, on your date, for your number of guests? Culinary Revolutionary serves South Florida's Gulf Coast across private chef dinners, event catering, weekly meal preparation and in-home cooking classes. Availability rests on three facts — service area, date and guest count — and confirming them early saves replanning later. Here is what each one involves.

1. Confirm the service area with your city or ZIP

The home kitchen, venue or event space must sit within the working service area on South Florida's Gulf Coast. Include your city or ZIP code in your very first message — it is the fastest way to confirm coverage. A complete street address is not needed at this stage; city or ZIP is enough to say yes or no.

If your gathering is at the edge of the usual area or involves a venue rather than a home, say so plainly. Venue kitchens, outdoor setups and club facilities each change the equipment picture, as the private chef versus catering guide explains. The earlier the location details are known, the better the service recommendation fits.

2. Lead with your date and how fixed it is

Dates decide faster than any other detail. State your date up front, and say whether it can move. A fixed wedding date gets a different answer than a birthday dinner with three possible weekends. Weekends and holiday periods book out first; weekday gatherings often have more room.

Different services want different lead times. A private dinner can often be arranged with a few weeks of notice. Catered events need more runway for tastings, rentals, staffing and venue coordination — typically six to eight weeks at minimum. Weekly meal preparation and cooking classes arrange around a recurring or single-session schedule instead. If your date is close, lead with it and ask what is still possible rather than planning the menu first.

3. Give an honest guest count, including the edge cases

Guest count chooses the service. Up to about twelve, a private chef dinner usually works best; past twenty or so, full catering takes over; between twelve and twenty, either can fit. An approximate count is fine at first — a range like fourteen to eighteen is more useful than a guess stated as fact — but be honest about who counts: vendors, plus-ones and children who need meals all change the plan.

A plan built for fifteen that becomes twenty-five is a different service, with different staffing and format. If your count is still settling, say what is firm and what might shift. Browsing the sample menus helps here too: the Spanish Tapas direction flexes across group sizes, while coursed menus like Italian Feast suit a known table.

4. Put it together in one inquiry

A complete first message names your city or ZIP, your date and whether it can move, your guest count or range, and which service you have in mind — private chef, catering, meal preparation or cooking class. Add the style of evening you picture and any menu direction that caught your eye. That is enough to confirm availability and start shaping the plan.

Ready? Tell Culinary Revolutionary your date, city or ZIP, and guest count — and browse the sample menus while you wait for a reply.
