Seven Sample Menus: How to Start From One and Make It Yours

By Culinary Revolutionary · October 15, 2026 · 3 min read

Seven starting points, endless adjustments. Here is how to read the sample menus and shape one into your evening.

Culinary Revolutionary publishes seven sample menus so hosts have a concrete place to start: Italian Feast, Brunch, Tour of Spain, Key West Inspired, Wild Game, Spanish Tapas, and Vegetarian. Every one of them is customizable. Think of each as a tested direction, not a fixed order. This guide explains how to read them and how to turn one into your evening on South Florida's Gulf Coast.

1. What the seven menus show

Each sample menu on the menus page lists courses and dishes that work well together for a particular style of gathering. The Italian Feast leans into shared courses and familiar pacing. Brunch suits late-morning gatherings with a relaxed timeline. Tour of Spain and Spanish Tapas offer two different Spanish directions: one coursed, one built from smaller plates. Key West Inspired brings coastal flavors. Wild Game is the heartiest direction. Vegetarian shows how a produce-centered menu can anchor a full evening.

Browse all seven before settling. Note which descriptions sound like your group, not just your own taste. A menu that fits the occasion usually beats a menu that fits one person.

2. Pick a starting direction, not a final menu

Start with the format of your evening. A seated anniversary dinner points toward coursed menus like the Italian Feast or Tour of Spain. A mingling crowd points toward Spanish Tapas. A daytime celebration points toward Brunch. Households that already eat mostly produce can start directly at Vegetarian rather than treating it as a fallback.

Then check the practical constraints: guest count, kitchen setup, and any dietary needs. A direction that matches your constraints is a better start than a favorite that fights them. If two menus both appeal, name both when you inquire — the final plan often borrows a course from each.

3. How customizing works in practice

Customization usually means swaps and adjustments, not starting from zero. Common changes include swapping one course for another from a different sample menu, adjusting a dish for a restriction, changing the number of courses to fit the timeline, or shifting portions for a longer or shorter evening. The sample dishes show the style and level of each menu; the final version reflects your group.

Dietary needs shape the customization early. Flag restrictions with your starting direction rather than after it — a vegetarian guest, a shellfish allergy, or a gluten restriction changes which courses carry over cleanly. Menus like the Vegetarian direction and coastal dishes from Key West Inspired often provide the most flexible base when restrictions are involved.

4. What to send when you inquire

A useful inquiry names four things: the sample menu or two you are starting from, your date, your city or ZIP code on South Florida's Gulf Coast, and your guest count. Add dietary needs and the style of evening you picture — seated courses, relaxed grazing, or daytime gathering. That is enough to confirm availability and shape a first menu draft.
