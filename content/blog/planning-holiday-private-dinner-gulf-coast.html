Holiday private dinners book the calendar early, and the relaxed ones are planned before the season gets busy. A seasonal gathering at home has its own shape: guests with fixed travel dates, a table that mixes ages and appetites, and a kitchen working through an evening everyone wants to enjoy. Culinary Revolutionary serves holiday dinners as customizable sample menus — including the family-style Italian Feast direction — for gatherings on South Florida's Gulf Coast. Here is how to plan one that stays relaxed from the first course to the last goodbye.

1. Start earlier than feels necessary

Holiday dates are the first to fill. Reach out as soon as your window firms up, even if the guest count is still a range and the menu is undecided. An early inquiry names the week, the city or ZIP code and the rough size of the table; everything else can be shaped after availability is confirmed. Waiting until every detail is settled often means settling for a leftover date instead.

Lock the pieces that affect other people first. Travel plans, visiting family and anyone sharing hosting duties all depend on the date, so confirm the evening before debating dishes. The availability guide covers what to confirm and when.

2. Pick a menu direction that fits the table

Holiday tables mix generations and tastes, so the menu direction should welcome the cautious eater and satisfy the adventurous one. The Italian Feast direction is built for exactly that kind of evening: antipasti to graze on while everyone arrives, primi and secondi that feel celebratory without demanding unfamiliar flavors, and dolci to close. Dishes from the Tour of Spain direction can add a festive note for guests who want one, and the Vegetarian direction keeps the table inclusive when several guests skip meat.

Flag dietary needs with your starting direction, not after it. A gluten restriction, a dairy allergy or a vegetarian guest changes which courses carry the evening, and the dietary planning guide covers what to share and when. If one guest's needs shape the whole table, say so early — it is a normal part of menu planning, not a complication.

3. Pace the evening around the celebration

Holiday dinners share the evening with everything around them: gifts, toasts, kids' bedtimes, a game on in the background. Tell the chef what else the evening holds so the courses pace around it rather than competing with it. A table with young children eats earlier and faster; a table of adults lingering over wine wants unhurried gaps. The celebration sets the rhythm, and the menu follows it.

Name the moments that matter when you inquire: the toast before dessert, the dish everyone expects each year, the guest arriving late from work who should not miss the main course. A private chef dinner adapts to the program; the service-choice guide walks through where a private chef evening ends and event catering begins, if your guest count pushes past a home table.

4. What to send when you inquire

A useful holiday inquiry names the Italian Feast direction or whichever sample menu caught your eye, your date or week, your city or ZIP code, your guest count or range, and the shape of the evening — kids included, late arrivals expected, traditions to honor. Add dietary needs and whether the gathering centers on one big table or a grazing setup.
