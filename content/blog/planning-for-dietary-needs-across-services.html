Dietary needs are planned in, not worked around. Whether the evening is a plated private dinner, a catered event or a week of prepared meals, restrictions and eating styles shape the menu from the start at Culinary Revolutionary — not as last-minute substitutions. This guide covers what to share, when to share it, and how each service on South Florida's Gulf Coast adapts.

1. Share the need, the severity and the headcount

A useful note names three things: the restriction itself, how strict it is, and how many guests it covers. A shellfish allergy that closes a kitchen to shellfish is different from a preference one guest mentions in passing; a table where half the guests eat vegetarian needs a different plan than a table with one. Medical allergies, intolerances, religious observances and chosen eating styles all count — and all are easier to serve well when described plainly.

Send this information with your first inquiry, alongside your date, city or ZIP and guest count. Retrofitting a finished menu narrows the options; starting with the constraints widens them. If needs are still coming in from guests, say that too — a menu built with flexibility beats one built twice.

2. How private dinners adapt

At a small private dinner, each guest gets a composed plate, so individual needs can be met course by course. A vegetarian guest receives a complete vegetarian plate rather than a main course with something missing. When restrictions affect one course, that course can be redesigned while the rest of the menu stands — the Italian Feast structure, for example, can carry a dietary adjustment in a single course without changing the evening.

Menus that already lean produce-centered make the strongest starting points for mixed tables. The Vegetarian direction often serves a broader group than hosts expect, and coastal dishes from Key West Inspired adapt cleanly to several common restrictions. Name your starting menu and your restrictions together when you inquire, as the sample-menus guide recommends.

3. How catering and meal preparation adapt

At catered events, guests with restrictions need a labeled option that is genuinely satisfying — not an afterthought plate. Flag needs early so the catering format (plated, stations or buffet, per the service-choice guide) can carry them: stations can dedicate a dish, buffets can label clearly, plated service can pace an alternate plate with the main courses.

For weekly meal preparation, share both restrictions and the broader eating style you want for the week — lighter proteins, more produce, specific exclusions. A vegetarian direction can shape the whole week, and the planning details in the meal-preparation booking guide (portions, storage, scheduling) apply with restrictions layered in from day one.

4. Put needs in your inquiry

List every restriction with its severity and headcount, note your service and starting menu direction, and include your date, city or ZIP and guest count. One message with all of it lets the menu be designed once, correctly.
